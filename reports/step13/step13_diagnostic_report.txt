Q1. Did any candidate meaningfully improve validation error?
    - Under the predefined Metric Improvement Gate (requiring WAPE imp >= 2%, MAE imp >= 2%, RMSE imp >= 1%,
      |Bias| <= 10 units, and RelBias <= 7.5%), NO candidate passed all criteria.
    - Step 12 HGB-L1-E1-R2 improved WAPE by +3.44% relative (-2.28 pp, to 63.83%) and MAE by +3.44% relative
      (-3.07 units, to 86.02), but failed RMSE (111.95 vs <= 107.61 required) and failed Bias (-26.80 units / 19.88% rel bias).
    - Step 12 HGB-R1 improved all L2 metrics marginally (MAE=89.07, RMSE=108.67, R²=+0.0001, Bias=+1.81), well below the 2% gate.

Q2. Did any candidate demonstrate genuine dynamic variance explanation?
    - NO. Zero candidates passed the Dynamic Variance Gate (0.50 <= STD Ratio <= 1.50, Corr >= 0.30, R² >= 0.10).
    - Across all ML candidates, maximum Pearson correlation is +0.01951 (HGB-R1) and maximum R² is +0.0001.
    - Even raw un-compressed historical baselines (Lag-1, Lag-7) have negative correlation (-0.0170 and -0.0220) and R² < -1.03.

Q3. Did predictions remain compressed?
    - YES. Every regularized ML candidate is severely compressed (STD Ratio = 0.0125 for HGB-L1-E1-R2, 0.0146 for HGB-R1,
      and 0.0286 for Ridge, all far below the 0.25 severe-compression boundary and the 0.50 Dynamic Gate threshold).

Q4. Is there measurable temporal demand signal?
    - Limited / near-zero temporal autocorrelation was detected in the historical target series.
    - Across all 100 Store x Product series in TRAIN (585 days each), mean autocorrelation at lags 1, 3, 7, 14, 21, and 30
      lies between -0.0038 and +0.0054, with group STD ~ 0.041 (identical to theoretical white-noise error 1/sqrt(585) = 0.0413).

Q5. Is there evidence of Store x Product-specific signal?
    - Store x Product expanding means have a tiny positive correlation in TRAIN (+0.0082), but across the 100 groups in
      validation, 0 out of 100 groups achieved STD Ratio >= 0.50 and only 1 out of 100 groups reached Correlation >= 0.30
      (expected by random chance across 100 series of length 73).

Q6. Is TRAIN -> VALIDATION distribution shift present?
    - NO. Target distribution shift is negligible (TRAIN Mean = 136.61, STD = 109.09 vs VALIDATION Mean = 134.78, STD = 108.69;
      PSI = 0.0019, KS = 0.0135). Behavioral lag/rolling features also show near-zero shift (PSI < 0.02).

Q7. Are residuals systematically biased by demand range, time, store, product, or Store x Product?
    - Residuals are NOT biased across time (August, September, October validation MAE ~ 88.6–89.8), stores, or products.
    - However, residuals ARE strongly and systematically biased by ACTUAL DEMAND RANGE: because models predict a flat constant
      band (~136.6 for L2, ~108.0 for L1), low demand (0–99, actual mean 47.05) is systematically overpredicted by +61 to +90 units,
      while high demand (300–400+, actual mean 341–430) is systematically underpredicted by -205 to -322 units.

Q8. Does the evidence justify another controlled forecasting-model experiment?
    - NO. Because Experiments 1, 2, 9, and 12 prove that pre-timestamp historical features and within-series lags exhibit
      near-zero correlation (|r| <= 0.011) with day-t Units Sold, prediction compression is an optimal mathematical response
      to high irreducible day-to-day variance (STD ~ 109 units), NOT a model hyperparameter defect.

Q9. What specific limitation of the available dataset/features is supported by the diagnostics?
    - Limited dynamic predictive signal was detected under the tested leakage-safe feature and model configurations:
      specifically, once same-day contemporaneous variables (Demand Forecast, same-day Inventory Level, same-day Price/Discount)
      are properly excluded to prevent target leakage, daily store-product demand behaves as a high-variance stationary stochastic
      process (Mean ~ 136, STD ~ 109, CV ~ 0.80) without pre-timestamp autocorrelation or cross-sectional regime separation.
    - RECOMMENDED NEXT STEP: Conclude model tuning and proceed to Inventory Optimization (Safety Stock, Reorder Point, and
      Service-Level Buffering) explicitly designed to absorb the ~108.7-unit residual demand uncertainty around the unbiased
      L2 baseline forecast (HGB-R1 / Ridge) alongside the L1 median benchmark (HGB-L1-E1-R2).
